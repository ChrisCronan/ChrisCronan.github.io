Hi, I’m Chris.

I’ve been leading people for more than 25 years. I’m an engineer by trade, I’m happiest outside, and the part of the job I love most is watching someone grow to the point where they don’t need me anymore.

Pull up a chair. Here’s a little about me and how I think about leading people.

Read my story or just say hello

How I got here

I didn’t start out leading anybody. I started out fixing things.

My first jobs were as a robotics and field service engineer at Stäubli and DNS Electronics. A lot of that was standing next to a machine that wasn’t doing what it was supposed to, figuring out why. It’s a good way to learn to find the real problem before you start talking about fixes.

I served in the U.S. Marine Corps from 2003 to 2008, and I have an MS in Engineering Management from Ohio University. At Honda, I led new technology and new model programs. Today I’m an NPI & Technical Program Portfolio Leader at Tesla.

Somewhere along the way I figured out the machines were the easy part. The people are the real work. They’re also the best part. My faith grounds how I try to treat people and how I try to serve them.

I’m not a guru. I’m an engineer who ended up leading people and decided to take it seriously. Different places, same lesson: the work gets better when the people doing it get better.

What I believe about leading people

Nothing fancy. Just what’s held up for me.

  1. Train yourself out of the job.

    If my team can’t run without me, I haven’t done my job. I want to develop people until they don’t need me, then go find the next thing to work on. Everyone starts as rough stone. A good leader helps shape it, and keeps working on his own.

  2. Be firm, not arrogant.

    Hold the standard. Say the hard thing when it needs saying. You can do all of that without being a jerk, and without needing to be the smartest guy in the room.

  3. Lead by serving the people you lead.

    My job is to clear the way and give my team what they need, not the other way around. You can push a team hard with brute force for a while. It doesn’t last. Mentoring, a clear process, and somebody in your corner last a lot longer.

  4. Patience pays off.

    I can sit in a deer stand for hours and be perfectly happy. That same patience helps with people. Most growth doesn’t happen on your schedule.

Outside of work

Give me a trail, an open road, or work to do around the ranch, and I’m good.

I hike and hunt. I run and I cycle. I ride motorcycles, on the street and on the motocross track. A lot of my weekends go to work around the ranch, which is never really finished, and I don’t mind. Some of my best mornings start early, working out with a group of guys before the sun is up.

Fair warning: I can sit for hours on a hunt, but hand me a fishing pole and I’m bored before the bobber settles.

Faith, time outdoors with my family, and those early mornings keep me grounded. The outdoors also keeps me honest. The weather doesn’t care about your plan. Ranch work doesn’t get done by talking about it. And a long, quiet morning is a good place to think about the people I’m responsible for.

Things I’ve built

I’m still an engineer at heart. Here are a couple of things I’ve had my hands on.

  • Patent application

    Vacuum attachment for removing debris from automotive bodies

    I’m a co-inventor on a Honda patent application for a vacuum attachment that pulls weld spatter and fine debris off vehicle bodies before paint, so the finish comes out clean. Great paint starts before the paint.

    U.S. Patent Application Publication No. US 2021/0282609 A1 · Honda Motor Co., Ltd. · Published Sept. 16, 2021

  • Safety systems

    Hydrogen fuel cell line safety system

    I designed, installed, and commissioned the gas detection and safety alarm system for a hydrogen fuel cell manufacturing line: flammable gas alarms, warning and normal status lights, emergency stop, and gas interlocks for each bay.

    Simple to say, serious to get right: keep the people on the line safe.
